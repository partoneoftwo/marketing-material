01 Joiners, movers, leavers

HR says it once. Every system follows.

Your HR system is the source of truth. midPoint reads it, works out which roles each person is born with from department, location and job, and creates, changes or disables the accounts in the systems people sign in to. Nobody has to raise a ticket for the basics.

  • Birthright roles assigned automatically from HR attributes, and removed when they no longer apply
  • Accounts in Active Directory, Entra ID, LDAP, databases and applications through midPoint's connectors
  • Preview a change with midPoint's simulations before it touches a real account
  • midPoint is the open-source identity governance platform from Evolveum, and stays a separate system you can inspect

02 Risk signals from midPoint

The risk signals come straight from what midPoint records.

Many Worlds Identity does not guess. It reads midPoint's audit trail, the results of its policy rules, the situation of every account after reconciliation, role analysis and certification decisions, with an account that can read and nothing more. The reading side never changes a user, an account or a role.

  • Audit trail: who changed what, on whose behalf, through which channel, and whether it succeeded, from the audit tables or the audit log file
  • Policy rules: segregation of duties breaches, roles without an owner, too many or too few holders of a role
  • Reconciliation: accounts with no owner, two owners or more than one possible owner
  • Role analysis: users whose access does not look like their peers'
  • Certification: decisions to revoke that were never carried out, and reviews nobody answered

03 Can these signals be trusted?

The report starts by saying how completely each system could be read.

A system that looks clean because it was never reconciled must never read as a system with nothing wrong. So every report opens with which systems midPoint has read, when, and how many accounts it could not place, and says whether the figures below are complete or lower bounds.

  • Systems connected, and when each was last reconciled
  • Reconciliation tasks that failed, and the error they stopped on
  • If a source cannot be read, the report still builds and says what is missing

04 Findings, ranked by severity

Each finding comes with why it matters, what to do and the evidence.

A finding is something about who has access that needs a decision. The worst come first, and each one names the midPoint record behind it, so it can be checked by anyone with access to midPoint.

  • Accounts with no owner, toxic combinations, leavers with live access, changes made by hand instead of from HR
  • Trends against the report before, so you can see whether risk is going up or down
  • PDF, Excel and PowerPoint from every page

05 One report, three readers

Leadership and identity owners ask different questions. Each gets its own view.

The executive summary answers in a page. The identity owner view goes down to the single account and the audit record behind it. The full report carries the evidence for whoever does the work.

Questions for leadership

  • Is identity risk going up or down?
  • Do leavers lose their access on the last day?
  • How many accounts does nobody own?
  • Who holds administrator rights, and who approved them?
  • Where can one person both do and approve?
  • Are access reviews done, and done on time?
  • Which systems are not under control yet?
  • Where do we stand against the access controls in ISO 27001 and NIS2?

Questions for identity owners

  • Which accounts are unmatched, disputed or in collision?
  • Which provisioning operations failed, and why?
  • Which roles have no owner?
  • Which assignments break a segregation of duties rule?
  • Which users have access their peers do not?
  • What changed in the audit trail since the report before?
  • Which changes were made by hand instead of from HR?
  • Which review decisions were never carried out?

06 Outliers

See who has access that their peers do not.

midPoint's role analysis groups people with similar access and points out the ones who do not fit: a customer service agent who can approve payments, a developer with administrator rights in production. midPoint only suggests. Many Worlds Identity puts each suggestion where a person decides on it.

  • Uses the role mining and outlier detection built into midPoint
  • Each outlier shows the peer group it was measured against
  • A suggestion, never an automatic removal: it becomes a review or a task

07 Access reviews

Reviews that close the loop, and proof that they did.

midPoint runs certification campaigns: managers and role owners confirm or revoke the access of the people they answer for. Many Worlds Identity reads the outcome and shows what an auditor asks for: what was reviewed, what was revoked, and whether the revoking actually happened.

  • Scheduled campaigns, and reviews started on their own by a policy rule after a move
  • A revoke that was decided but not carried out is a finding
  • Unanswered is never shown as kept

08 Conclusions

The report ends in three lists somebody can act on.

Risks go to a register, incidents to a SOC, tasks to a queue. Every rating carries the rule that produced it, because these are first readings from what midPoint recorded and the page says so.

  • Send the three lists to a SpaceRisk register with one button. Nothing is sent until it is pressed
  • Sending again updates what was sent before and never makes a second copy
  • Exports for a SIEM (OCSF) and for Jira, ServiceNow or a mailbox, plus Excel and PowerPoint

For the risk register

Risks

A standing weakness, such as a toxic combination or a role without an owner, rated likelihood times consequence, each from 1 to 5, and given to an owner.

For the SOC

Incidents

Something that needs responding to now, such as a leaver who can still sign in.

For a queue

Tasks

One piece of work, such as decommissioning an account, with a queue, a due date and a way to tell when it is finished.
